# The Basel problem

In 1734 a young Leonhard Euler summed the reciprocals of the squares, a question that had beaten the Bernoullis for half a century:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}
$$

Nobody expected $\pi$ in a sum of whole numbers. Euler found it by treating $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ as a polynomial with a root at every multiple of $\pi$, and comparing the two ways of writing it.

The same trick gives every even power: $\zeta(4) = \frac{\pi^4}{90}$, $\zeta(6) = \frac{\pi^6}{945}$, and on up the line.
